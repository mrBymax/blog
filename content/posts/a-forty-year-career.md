# The American Dream (What happened to it?)

I really believe that every person can attain success, prosperity and social advancement, despite its birthplace, family status etc.
I've really interiorised this concept and I still dream about a word in which I can express myself at best. I still don't know where my career is heading, if it's financial independence or personal success or another thing. 

My parents are pretty "adults", and they will continue working for at least 10 years (shame on _SPNI_), and while I am still trying to find the motivation to finish my CS degree (which I am not lacking because of my love for Computer Science, which is stronger than ever, but because of issues with my University that are "unlikely to be resolved"), I am also harshly looking for some things to do for a living.

## Some data

During the last two months, I've applied for $90$ jobs, receiving a total of $44$ rejection, $25$ ghosting, $10$ OAs and $0$ interviews (yet).
Which is (almost) better than the majority of the people. I've read a lot of blog posts in which people said that they applied to more than 100 jobs receiving 2 positive responses.

## What's the problem?

> The job market is cooked.

But, is it? Maybe. We're living in a difficult time, in which people with jobs don't want to lose it, and people looking for them, are desperate.
Aligning with the article, I've lost the 40 years model, in "favour" of the 50+ years model. 
Reading about past experiences from success people, I feel like I've developed a strong [survivorship bias](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/survivorship-bias) which leads to nowhere.

What should I do?

In Italian, we have an interesting expression: "to listen to every bell tower", which could be translated to "if you want to have a solid grasp of everything, and make yourself a good idea, you should listen to multiple ideas (and it's even better if these ideas do not agree with each other)".

Some say: "just wait, finish your degree, good times will come". Other say "you're wasting time, you're a complete failure. X at your age founded a 1B+ company and you're still **dreaming** about a job".

What should I listen to? I feel like both of these bell towers are right. I mean, if I were good enough, I could have solved an important problem and made an exit. 
But if I do not "trust the process", I could drop off before the "good times".

The truth stands in the middle.

## The Proposed Strategy

### Pace

This is easy. Too fast? Burnout is granted. Too slow? No advancement.
The goal is to have a _sustainable_ pace. Work is a marathon, not a sprint. Energy management is **essential** for mental health, and ignoring it would damage your life (it inevitably reflects on your daily life). And even the quality resents from the lack of mental health.

> There's no simple formula, it depends on obligations and environment outside from work.

The most important thing is: **if you struggle with anxiety when you log out work, then the pace is too fast. This means that anxiety has impacted your job and your results**. You need to take a step back for success.
