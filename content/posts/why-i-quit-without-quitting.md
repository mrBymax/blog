An interesting story about broken ideals and dusted promises.
A goodbye (?) letter, to UniUD.

## OK cool, context?

Back in 2022, I obtained my diploma in Telecommunications and Computer Science in High School, after 2.5 years of COVID pandemic and lockdowns, 
I left high school, ready to crash uni, fulfilled with the desire of meeting an enormous amount of clever and smart people and to let them inspire me and to grow together with them. 

Needless to say, I was wrong.

What I obtained was a _tragedy_ (OK, maybe defining it a "tragedy" is too much, but I'll explain this point later)

## University's Goal is not to Learn

I reached this conclusion recently, and I have spent quite some time trying to validate it.
I looked online, talked to people, even professors. The answer is not easy and this intrigues me very much.
In reality, there's not a correct answer.

## Learning how to gain the right Motivation is the key to survive, but watch out, the Results are not good.

What shifted when I found out that results are truly performance-dependant and not preparation-dependant, my mentality shifted. I found out that while my colleagues where there, trying to hack their way into exams, memorizing past papers and answers to a provided questions paper, I needed to do better. To learn for real.

There's the paradox. I've seen people acing exams with astonishing results (30/30, or with laude) that do not know how to do basic stuff, or that do not know basic definitions (I've also argued with a couple of them because they got a greater mark even with those premises). 
And, of course, the high-school paradox in which I tend to help people that gain better grades than me. Absurd.

## The Things I Like

> _Ah yes, I see! You do the things you like excellently, but the things you don't like..._

That's what one of the best professors I've ever had, told me the second time I went visiting him. And he was **very** right. 

Things have not changed. There are things that I love doing, that I love exploring, that I do well, and things that I don't like, or I don't find useful or even _bad_ (but that I have to do), way less good.

Why so this paragraph is about motivation?

Because the main motivation I got was from the desire of knowledge. This moved everything.
